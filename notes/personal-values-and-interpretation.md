# Personal Values & Interpretation Caveats

> **Important limitation:** everything below is an interpretation from
> observed conversation patterns, not verified fact, diagnosis, or
> prediction. An AI using this file should describe observable patterns
> ("often prefers concise explanations") and never present them as
> certainties ("has personality type X") or extend them into territory
> never explicitly shared: private thoughts, exact emotional/mental
> state, diagnoses, future outcomes, relationships, family, finances,
> politics, or religion.

## What seems to matter
Building something real (not just studying theory), being technically
capable enough to answer for it (especially under interview pressure),
making projects impressive for competitions/demonstrations, solving
practical problems, understanding technology rather than copying it,
enough independence to test and continue without hand-holding, and
getting engineering work seen (competitions, exhibitions, LinkedIn,
portfolios).

## How decisions get made
Weighs buildability, availability, affordability, whether it solves the
actual problem, and whether it can be demonstrated and explained — not
just theoretical component superiority. See
[[problem-solving-and-debugging-style]] for the component-selection
pattern in more detail.

## Idea generation pattern
Tends to ask "can this technology be used for something else?" rather
than only "what is this normally used for?" — an associative,
cross-domain style (e.g. thermal sensor → human detection → AI → ESP32 →
HVAC → energy efficiency → product → competition). Useful for innovation;
the flip side is many ideas can surface at once, so prioritization matters.

## Reactions under difficulty
Frustration tends to surface when an answer doesn't match what was asked,
a circuit misbehaves unexpectedly, or a response is longer than needed —
the useful response is to correct the misunderstanding and move forward,
not add reassurance. The typical arc is problem → frustration →
clarification → another attempt → testing → correction → working result;
iteration continues rather than the project being abandoned.

## What "success" tends to mean
Progresses roughly: it works → I understand why it works → I can show it
to someone → I can make it better → I can turn it into something real.

## Engineering identity (as currently framed)
Best described as an embedded systems engineer in development with
strong interests in edge AI, intelligent sensing, computer vision, and
multidisciplinary product prototyping — not reducible to "AI student",
"electronics student", or "embedded engineer" alone. Directions that fit
the existing trajectory: embedded AI engineer, edge AI/intelligent
hardware engineer, robotics/automation engineer, biomedical/medical AI,
hardware product engineer (prototype → PCB → product), or R&D engineer.
This is a description of current trajectory, not a prediction or a
constraint on future choices.

## Why the all-of-me vault exists
Framed as building a persistent, structured, machine-readable interface
between Venuja and future AI systems — experience → documentation →
structure → connections → machine-readable context — so history,
decisions, and working style don't depend on any one conversation's
memory.
